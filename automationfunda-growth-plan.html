Where you actually stand

An honest read, because the plan depends on it.

Real advantages

  • A proven channel already works. 50 contracts, $50K+, 95% Job Success, Top Rated Plus. Most new agencies have none of this.
  • An almost-empty niche. Of 223 agencies scanned, exactly one competes in Google Apps Script. Against 100+ in general "AI automation".
  • Published prices. Only 27% of competitors publish any price. Buyers rank pricing as their #1 need.
  • Real proof. 14 case studies with numbers, 9 named testimonials with photos.
  • A site that converts. Two-step form, embedded calendar, lead tracking, instant alerts.

Real constraints

  • The domain has no search authority yet. Expect close to zero organic traffic for 3–6 months. This is physics, not effort.
  • Low prices need volume. At $299–999 you need roughly 8–12 projects a month to reach a solid income, so lead flow matters more than polish.
  • Three people, one brand. Three LinkedIn profiles posting beats one company page by a wide margin. Use that.
  • No email list. Every channel below also needs to feed a list, or you rebuild your pipeline from scratch every month.
The single most important sentence in this document: for the next 90 days, Upwork and direct outreach pay the bills; SEO and content are an investment that pays from month 4 onward. Do not swap that order, however tempting the content work feels.

Channel 1: Upwork 5–8 h/week · starts paying immediately

This is your cash engine for the next 90 days. The goal is not more Upwork work at $25/hr; it is higher-value Upwork work that moves to direct contracts.

Fix the profile first

  • Put the tagged site link in your profile, portfolio items and proposals (links are in automationfunda-outreach-links.html).
  • Rebuild your portfolio around the 14 case studies on the site, each linking to its live page. Buyers who click through see proof, prices and a booking calendar.
  • Raise your rate. You are at $25/hr; the market band for this work is $50–150/hr. Move to $45–60 now and to $75 once you have 3 new 5-star reviews at the higher rate.
  • Narrow the headline to the niche you actually win in: Google Workspace and Apps Script automation, with n8n and AI as secondary.

Proposal routine

  • 10–15 proposals a week, not 40. Quality beats volume because Upwork weights response rate.
  • Use your existing job-applicant skill to draft each one, then cut it to 150–200 words.
  • Open with the specific result, not your CV: "You're describing the same problem a campground in Alaska had. Their booking confirmations and invoices now run themselves — about 90% of the emails. Here's how I'd do yours."
  • Always include one relevant case study link. That is what the site is for.

Moving clients off-platform, legitimately

Upwork permits moving a relationship off-platform after 24 months, or earlier by paying the conversion fee. Do not breach their terms. Instead: deliver the first project on Upwork, then offer the care plan at $299/month. Recurring work is where your margin lives, and it is the natural bridge to a direct relationship when the time comes.

Channel 2: LinkedIn 3–4 h/week · first leads in 2–4 weeks

Personal profiles get far more reach than company pages, and you have three of them. This is your highest-leverage free channel.

Profile setup (once, 30 minutes each)

  • Rewrite each headline around the problem you solve, not the job title. For example: "We automate the spreadsheet work your team does by hand | Google Workspace & n8n | AutomationFunda".
  • Put the tagged site link in the Featured section and the website field on all three profiles.
  • Add a banner image that states the offer and the price floor.

Posting rhythm

3 posts a week each, from the personal profiles. Consistency beats volume: three a week every week beats ten in one week then silence. Reshare to the company page, never the reverse.

What actually works in 2026

  • Native documents, carousels and video beat link posts by a wide margin. Put the link in the first comment.
  • Write from real work. Obviously AI-written posts get ignored, and your advantage is that your material is real.
  • Reply to every comment within an hour or two. That is where conversations become calls.

Direct messages

Not pitches. When someone engages with a post, look at their profile, and if they fit, send something like:

Thanks for the comment on the invoicing post, [Name]. Out of curiosity — is that something your team still does by hand? I ask because the fix is usually smaller than people expect. No pitch either way. Happy to tell you what I'd do if it helps.

Target: 5–10 of these a week across all three profiles. Expect roughly 1 call per 15–20 messages.

Publishing rhythm

2 articles a month, each 1,200–2,000 words with real working code. That is enough to build authority without eating the week. Every article ends with a link to book the audit.

Repurpose each one: 1 article → 3 LinkedIn posts → 1 short video → 1 email to the list. Write once, use four times.

Off-page SEO and backlinks

Off-page work is about other sites vouching for you. For a new domain, a handful of real, relevant links beats hundreds of junk ones, and junk ones can actively hurt.

Start with the free, legitimate ones

  • Your own profiles: Upwork, LinkedIn (all three), Facebook, YouTube, GitHub. Each should link to the site. Do this first; it also confirms the sameAs data now in your schema.
  • Google Business Profile: you can register as a service-area business without showing an address. Free, and it helps brand searches.
  • Directories that matter in your niche: n8n's creator/expert listings, Make and Zapier partner directories, Clutch, GoodFirms. These pass real relevance.
  • Product and community profiles: Stack Overflow, Reddit (participate properly, never drop links), the Google Apps Script community, Indie Hackers.

Then earn the harder ones

  • Guest articles on automation and small-business blogs. Your real code and real numbers are genuinely publishable material, which most pitches lack.
  • Open-source a useful script. Publish the invoice-emailer or the de-duplication script on GitHub with a good README linking back. Developers link to tools they use.
  • Answer questions where your buyers are, with substance and no link. The profile link does the work.
  • Client case studies: ask the clients you build for to mention you. A link from a real business in your niche is worth dozens of directory entries.
Do not buy backlinks or use link-building packages. At your size, a penalty would cost more than the links could ever earn. Everything above is slower and permanent.

Channel 4: Cold email 3 h/week · 3–5 weeks before the first send

You already have a setup guide for this (Cold_Email_Domain_Setup_Guide.md). The important thing is that the clock starts weeks before the first email goes out.

5–10%what a well-targeted campaign gets
14+ daysminimum domain warm-up before sending
under 2%bounce rate you must stay below

Setup order

  1. Buy 2–3 separate sending domains (never your main one) and set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on each.
  2. Warm them up for at least 14 days before a single cold email. Properly warmed domains reach about 87% of inboxes; cold ones reach about 12%.
  3. Build the list by hand to start. 100 genuinely well-matched prospects beat 5,000 scraped ones.
  4. Send 20–30 a day per inbox, no more.

Who to target

Pick one industry you already have proof in and own it. Your strongest options, in order:

  • Campgrounds, RV parks and tour operators — you have the Fairbanks KOA story with real numbers.
  • Landscaping and home services — you have the estimator and commission system.
  • AV installers and equipment dealers — you have the D-Tools price book work with a named client.

The sequence that fits your offer

Email 1 (day 0) — the specific observation Subject: your booking confirmations Hi [Name], I build automations for campgrounds that run group bookings. Last season I built one for a KOA in Alaska — booking confirmations, PDF invoices, vendor emails and payment reminders now go out on their own. About 90% of the admin. Is that still a manual job at [Business]? Farhad — automationfunda.com Email 2 (day 4) — proof, no ask Short version of the case study with the real numbers. Link to the case study page. Email 3 (day 9) — the easy yes "Worth a 30-minute look at your booking process? No pitch — I'll tell you what's worth automating and what isn't." + booking link. Email 4 (day 16) — close the loop "I'll stop here. If it ever becomes a priority, the audit link stays open."
Legal note: cold email to businesses is lawful in the US under CAN-SPAM with a real address and working unsubscribe, and in the UK/EU under B2B rules, but the rules differ by country. Include an unsubscribe line and honour it immediately. Since you publish no physical address, use a mail-forwarding address for the sending domains.

Email marketing and nurture

Most people who visit are not ready today. Without a list you lose all of them. This is the highest-return thing most small agencies skip.

Build the list

  • Add one lead magnet to the site. The obvious one: a free Google Sheets template that does something genuinely useful, such as the invoice emailer from your own article. It proves the skill and collects the email.
  • Offer it at the end of every article and in a small bar on the site. Not a pop-up.
  • Everyone who books an audit and everyone who downloads the template joins the list.

Speed is the cheapest advantage you have. Leads contacted within 5 minutes are far more likely to convert than those contacted after 30. The average company takes 42 hours and 23% never reply at all. Simply answering fast puts you ahead of most of your competition.

How to run the audit call

  1. 0–10 min: they talk. Ask what the process is, who does it, how often, how long it takes. Write down the hours.
  2. 10–25 min: you talk. What you'd automate, which tool, what you'd leave alone. Name one thing that isn't worth automating — it buys more trust than anything else you can say.
  3. 25–30 min: "I'll send a fixed price and a delivery date by email today." Then actually do it.

Retention and referrals

Your own history proves this is your strongest asset: much of your work came from repeat clients, including one who kept you for 1,100+ hours.

  • Mine your past clients this week. You have ~35 completed contracts. Message every one: "We've expanded into a small team and now do X. Anything on your side eating hours?" Expect 2–4 conversations from 35 messages, at zero cost.
  • Ask for a referral at the moment of delight, which is right after handover when the thing works: "If you know anyone drowning in the same kind of admin, I'd appreciate an introduction."
  • Offer a referral reward, such as a free month of the care plan for both sides.
  • Ask for the review at the same moment. Every new named testimonial strengthens the site.

Most of month 1 and 2 revenue will come from Upwork and referrals, not the website. That is expected and fine.

The five numbers to check weekly

  1. Leads by source — already in your pipeline sheet (utm_source column). Tells you which channel to double down on.
  2. Lead → booked call rate — if below 30%, the problem is the form or the follow-up speed.
  3. Call → proposal rate — if below 70%, you are taking calls with the wrong people.
  4. Proposal → won rate — if below 30%, the price or the scope is wrong.
  5. Average project value — if this is not rising by month 3, your prices are too low.
Review monthly, not daily. With small numbers, daily changes are noise. Make one change at a time so you know what caused what.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for SEO. It will not produce meaningful traffic before month 4. If you treat it as your main channel now, you will have a quiet, polished quarter with no revenue.
  • Starting all channels at once and doing none properly. Upwork + LinkedIn + past clients first. Add cold email when the domains are warm.
  • Posting generic AI-written content. Your advantage is real projects with real numbers. Nobody else can write those posts.
  • Competing on price. You already set low prices to make the first yes easy. Do not go lower; raise them as reviews accumulate.
  • Chasing broad keywords. "AI automation agency" is unwinnable. "Email pdf from google sheets" is winnable this quarter.
  • Buying backlinks. The penalty risk outweighs any gain at your size.
  • Letting leads go cold. Your daily digest exists for exactly this. Reply the same day, every day.
  • Not asking for reviews. Every named testimonial makes the site work harder, forever.
