The short version

  • PLAN
    The strategy is sound, but the work is behind schedule. Channel order, niche and targets are right. Six weeks in, none of the plan's website tasks for weeks 1–6 are live: there are still 3 niche articles, no lead magnet, no schema, and the off-topic posts are still up with broken links.
  • SITE
    The site is fast, secure and full of real proof, but its structure works against you. Case studies can't be linked one at a time. Nothing captures an email. Pages don't link to each other. 8 of 11 blog posts pull away from your niche.
  • EMAIL
    Go US first, one industry at a time. Start with campgrounds and landscapers. Both are in their off-season now, which is when owners fix their systems. Before the first send, give each industry a page to land on.
  • TRUST
    Settle the numbers before a prospect finds two versions. The site says 95% JSS, 1,766 hours, 50+ projects. "My Upwork profile.txt" says 100% JSS, 9,700+ hours, 100 projects, and leads with Power BI. It matches Kazi Safayet's bio. If it is his profile, the site is under-selling the team. If it is Farhad's, one set of numbers is wrong.

The growth plan: keep and change

Keep

  • Upwork, past clients and LinkedIn first; SEO as a slow investment. Right for a new domain.
  • Owning Google Workspace and Apps Script instead of "AI automation".
  • Fast replies to leads, and audit calls that say what isn't worth automating.
  • No bought links. Prices on the site.
  • Honest 90-day targets that expect Upwork to pay the bills at first.

Change

Eight changes, listed below. The biggest: cut scope to what three people can run, and build industry landing pages before cold email starts.

  1. Cut the plan to what three people can run. The hours don't add up. Nine LinkedIn posts a week (three people × three) won't fit in 3–4 hours, and neither will hand-researching 100 prospects inside a 3-hour cold email slot. Give each channel one owner and one weekly number. For example: Farhad on Upwork, calls and proposals; Harun on the cold email system and LinkedIn; Safayet on dashboard content and BI work.
  2. Build industry pages and single case-study pages before cold email. The plan sends cold prospects to the general homepage. A campground owner should land on a page about campground booking automation with the KOA story on it, and Email 2 needs a real link to that story.
  3. Tighten the Europe legal note. "UK/EU B2B rules" is not one rule. The US and UK limited companies allow cold email with an opt-out. Several EU countries, including Germany, Austria and Denmark, need prior consent even for B2B email. Use LinkedIn there. Details are under Who to email.
  4. Keep past-client outreach inside Upwork. Upwork's terms bar taking clients you met there off-platform for 24 months unless you pay the conversion fee. Send the 35 past-client messages through Upwork and route new work through it. Losing that account would cost more than every other channel in the plan earns.
  5. Drop "FAQ schema to win extra space in search results". Since August 2023, Google has shown FAQ rich results only for well-known government and health sites. Article, Person and Service markup are better uses of that hour.
  6. Fix measurement before reading the numbers. The cookie banner applies EU opt-in to every visitor, so GA4 only counts people who click Accept, US visitors included. Add Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless, free and already on your host. Keep the /api/lead sheet as the source of truth for leads.
  7. Pick one front door. The Upwork profile leads with Power BI. The site leads with Google Workspace but has 10 service pages. The blog is mostly AI voice agents and GoHighLevel. A buyer who sees two of these sees two different companies.
  8. Price for the market you're emailing. "From $299" works as an Upwork entry point. For US cold outreach, quote after the audit instead of leading with price. A US agency would price a build like the three-phase KOA system in the low thousands. A top tier of "from $999" sets an anchor that is hard to move later.

Who to email, and where

Choose the market for legal reasons first, then the industry for proof.

Markets

MarketCold email?What it takes
United StatesStart hereCAN-SPAM: honest sender and subject, a physical postal address, and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days.
United KingdomLimited companies onlyPECR allows B2B cold email to companies if each email has an opt-out. Sole traders and partnerships count as individuals and need consent first.
Rest of EuropeCountry by countryRules differ. Several countries, including Germany, Austria and Denmark, need prior consent even for B2B email, so use LinkedIn there. Where cold email is allowed, GDPR also means stating where you got the person's details.

General guidance, not legal advice. Check a country's rules before you scale past a pilot there.

Setup and compliance

Sending setup

  • Two sending domains that are variations of your name, never the main domain. Two Google Workspace inboxes on each.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every domain. Warm up for at least 14 days.
  • A sending tool with warm-up and reply detection, such as Instantly or Smartlead (about $40/month).
  • Verify every address first with MillionVerifier, NeverBounce or ZeroBounce. Keep bounces under 2% and spam complaints under 0.3%.
  • Plain text, no images, no open-tracking pixel, no link in Email 1.

Build before the first send

  • A campground landing page with the KOA story, what you automate for parks, a price range and the audit form.
  • The KOA case study on its own URL.
  • A 2-minute walkthrough video of the KOA system, recorded with the client's permission or on demo data.
  • A registered postal address for the footer.
  • The same page, case study and video for landscaping before its sequence starts.
Keep the two lists separate. Cold prospects go in the sending tool. Only people who download the template or book an audit join the newsletter list. Mixing them is the fastest way to ruin the newsletter's sender reputation.
